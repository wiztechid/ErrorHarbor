Affiliate Disclosure

How ErrorHarbor uses affiliate links and keeps recommendations separate from troubleshooting guidance.

Last updated: September 2026.

How affiliate links work

Some ErrorHarbor pages may contain affiliate links. If you follow one of these links and make a qualifying purchase or complete another qualifying action, ErrorHarbor may receive a commission or referral fee. This should not increase the price you pay solely because you used our link.

Clear disclosure

Pages containing affiliate recommendations should include a clear disclosure close enough to the commercial recommendation for a reader to understand the relationship before acting on it.

Relevance rule

An affiliate product or service should appear only when it is contextually relevant to the problem, environment, prevention or migration path being discussed. A higher commission is not, by itself, a reason to recommend a product.

Editorial separation

Affiliate recommendations should remain distinguishable from Quick Fixes, command blocks, warnings and verification steps. An affiliate relationship does not determine whether ErrorHarbor describes a technical fix as appropriate or successful.

Advertising is separate

Display advertising and affiliate links are different monetization methods. An advertisement served by an advertising provider is not an ErrorHarbor editorial recommendation merely because it appears on the site.

Third-party terms and privacy

Affiliate merchants and networks operate under their own terms and privacy practices. Following an affiliate link may allow the destination or affiliate network to receive referral information needed to attribute the visit or purchase. See our Privacy Policy for additional information.

Questions

Questions about an affiliate disclosure may be sent to info@errorharbor.com.
