Editorial Methodology

How ErrorHarbor researches, writes, reviews and updates technical troubleshooting pages.

Reader-first standard

Every core guide is built to answer two questions quickly: “Am I on the right page?” and “What is the safest useful thing to try first?” Background information is included when it helps the reader diagnose or fix the problem, not simply to make a page longer.

How guides are structured

  1. Match the problem. Identify the exact error, product, symptom and relevant environment.
  2. Start with lower-risk checks. Prefer reversible or easily verified actions before destructive changes.
  3. Explain the action. Commands and settings should have enough context for the reader to understand what they affect.
  4. Verify the result. Tell the reader what success looks like and what to check after a meaningful change.
  5. Branch when necessary. If the first fix does not work, move into likely causes and environment-specific diagnosis rather than repeating the same advice.
  6. Separate evidence types. Vendor documentation, editorial explanation and community reports should be distinguishable when that difference matters.

Research and source handling

When a solution depends on product behavior, version-specific requirements or a documented command, we aim to check relevant primary or authoritative technical documentation where available. Community reports can help identify real-world variants, but a repeated forum suggestion is not automatically treated as verified fact.

Safety and clarity

Troubleshooting can involve configuration, permissions, services, virtual machines, development environments or stored data. Guides should identify meaningful risk where practical, avoid requesting sensitive credentials, and favor backups or reversible steps when a change could have material consequences.

Quality controls

Priority pages are expected to meet an internal Article Quality Score before publication. We check for intent match, actionable troubleshooting, verification, unnecessary duplication and internal-link context. We do not fabricate testing, solved rates, user outcomes or community consensus.

Updates

Guides may be reviewed when software behavior changes, a documented requirement changes, readers report a reproducible problem, or search behavior indicates that the page no longer answers the dominant troubleshooting intent. Material corrections should improve the existing guide rather than leave known misleading instructions in place.

Corrections & updates

If you find an incorrect command, unsafe recommendation, outdated version note, broken link or materially misleading explanation, email info@errorharbor.com. Include the affected page URL, what appears incorrect and, when possible, the product/version involved. Do not send passwords, API keys, access tokens or other private credentials. See the Corrections Policy for additional details.
